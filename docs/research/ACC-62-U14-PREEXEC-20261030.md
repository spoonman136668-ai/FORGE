## Narrow falsifiable universal claim
For **arbitrary** independently selected N-byte weight tensors, a fixed lossless decoder and a strictly shorter (<8N bit) complete representation (procedure+seed+side metadata) cannot represent **all** 2^(8N) possible source tensors. The number of possible bit strings with length less than 8N is 2^(8N)-1, assuming the same decoder and no uncharged external information. The pigeonhole obstruction applies even with unlimited time; it does NOT prohibit a short generator for weights that were created by that generator, compression of structured tensors, approximation, a learned shared codebook, or benefits for a restricted distribution. Decoder or learned model bytes must count when not fixed/shared fairly.

**Counterexample to the universal claim:** Enumerate 1-byte and 2-byte source domains, with a decoder using a 1-byte seed: at most 256 of 65,536 two-byte weights can have unique 1-byte seed codes. For all variable-length encodings shorter than 16 bits, there are exactly 65,535 possible codewords for 65,536 distinct 2-byte tensors. Any universal lossless scheme either retains an >=16-bit representation for at least one source, uses external side information, or loses exactness.

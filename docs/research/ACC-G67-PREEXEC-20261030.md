## Specific falsifiable hypothesis and mechanism

Load the **same original authenticated checkpoint** as standard `torch.float16`, SDPA, on CUDA, then **only** convert `lm_head.weight` to float32 and compute `F.linear(hidden_states.float(), head_weight.float(), optional_bias)` in float32. Return true float32 logits without recasting to half. Keep embeddings, all 22 backbone blocks, KV states, hidden states and SDPA kernels in FP16. Use a small wrapper for the output head to avoid PyTorch mixed-input/weight dtype errors; verify backbone dtypes and actual SDPA operator after conversion. Reject unexpected tied embedding/head parameters, quantization, autocast or other architectural change. Convert the original FP16-loaded projection weights; do not claim this restores original pre-half-quantized weights.

**Primary hypothesis:** the final-head FP32 projection may remove the near-tie without the approximately 2× whole-model GPU allocation of G66 full FP32. **Counter-hypothesis:** the FP16 backbone already loses enough information that head-only FP32 cannot restore canonical decoding agreement; or the head conversion makes actual latency/memory unacceptable. Do not select a different layer, apply per-token tie overrides, change generation logic, select prompts based on outcomes, or retune on this result.
